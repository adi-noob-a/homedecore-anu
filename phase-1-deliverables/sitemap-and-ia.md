### 2.2 CORE MATERIALS (`/materials`)

#### 2.2.1 Plywood Grades (`/materials/plywood-grades`)
**PDF Sections Mapped:**
- "What is Plywood?" — full definition, multi-layer structure
- "MR Plywood (Moisture Resistant)" — full section
  - Also called commercial plywood
  - Handles normal indoor moisture, not direct water
  - Used in: wardrobes, beds, shelves, indoor furniture, wall paneling
  - Limitations: not for kitchens/bathrooms, water damage risk
- "BWR Plywood (Boiling Water Resistant)" — full section
  - More water-resistant than MR, not as strong as BWP
  - Used in: kitchen dry zones, utility areas, semi-wet spaces
  - Handles occasional water exposure
- "BWP Plywood (Boiling Water Proof)" — full section
  - Highest grade, continuous water exposure
  - Waterproof adhesive, no swelling/cracking/delamination
  - Pest resistant, highly durable
  - Used in: kitchens, bathrooms, sink units, utility areas, outdoor furniture
- "TIP" section: use MR for dry areas (wardrobes, living/dining, bedrooms), BWP for kitchens/utility/bathrooms, BWR or BWP for kitchen wall units and lofts

**Grade Reference Table:**
| Grade | Designation | Moisture Level | Best For |
|-------|-------------|----------------|----------|
| MR Grade 303 | Moisture Resistant | Normal indoor | Wardrobes, beds, shelves, dry areas |
| BWR Grade 303 | Boiling Water Resistant | Moderate moisture | Kitchen dry zones, utility, semi-wet |
| BWP Grade 710 | Boiling Water Proof | Continuous water | Kitchens, bathrooms, sink units, outdoor |

#### 2.2.2 Engineered Boards (`/materials/engineered-boards`)
**PDF Sections Mapped:**

**HDHMR:**
- Two full HDHMR sections (overview + detailed)
- Made from tightly compressed wood fibers
- Stronger and tougher than MDF
- Handles moisture well, termite resistant, doesn't bend easily
- Smooth, paint-ready surface
- Used in: kitchens, bathroom vanities, wardrobes, humid area furniture
- Not for outdoor use

**MDF:**
- "MDF (Medium Density Board)" full section
- "MDF VS HDHMR" full comparison section
- Made from fine wood fibres compressed into smooth boards
- Very smooth finish, easy to cut and shape
- Used in: TV units, wardrobes (dry areas), wall panels, decorative designs
- Limitations: swells with water, weaker than plywood/HDHMR
- Best for painted furniture in dry areas only
- For CNC work (jaali panels, cut designs) only — not carcasses
- Low moisture resistance and poor screw-holding capacity

**Particle Board:**
- "What is a Particle Board?" full section
- Made from compressed wood chips and sawdust bonded with glue
- Low cost, smooth surface for laminates
- Not strong — weaker than plywood and MDF
- Not water resistant — swells with moisture
- Suitable only for: dry areas, light-use, low-budget furniture
- Avoid in: kitchens, bathrooms, heavy-load furniture

**Blockboard:**
- "Blockboard" full section
- Solid wooden strips inside, covered with plywood on both sides
- Light yet strong, bends less, holds screws well
- Cost-effective for large panels
- Used in: doors, long shelves, shutters, beds, tables, wardrobes (dry areas)
- Limitations: poor moisture resistance, can warp if low quality
- Avoid kitchens, bathrooms, wet areas

#### 2.2.3 Plywood Thickness Guide (`/materials/plywood-thickness`)
**PDF Sections Mapped:**
- "Plywood Thickness (Commonly Used in Interiors)" — full table
- 6mm: Back panels, drawer bottoms, partitions
- 9mm: Shutters, light panels, wall cladding
- 12mm: Shutters, shelves (light to medium load)
- 16-19mm: Carcass for kitchens, wardrobes, base units (STANDARD)
- 25mm: Counter supports, heavy-duty shelves, loft bases

### 2.3 SURFACE FINISHES (`/finishes`)

#### 2.3.1 Sheet Finishes (`/finishes/sheet-finishes`)

**Laminate:**
- "Laminate Sheets" full section
- "Common Types of Surface/Sheet Finishes" introduction
- "Matte vs Glossy Laminate" full comparison section
- Definition: thin decorative layers from paper and resin, pasted on plywood/MDF/HDHMR/particle board
- Properties: affordable, scratch-resistant, easy to clean, wide colours/textures
- Types: Matte, Glossy, Textured/Wood-Finish, High-Gloss, Exterior, Anti-Finger/Soft-Touch
- Matte: subtle, elegant, hides fingerprints, ideal for busy kitchens
- Glossy: bright, reflective, shows fingerprints, needs more cleaning
- "Slightly more visible joints compared to acrylic"
- Budget-friendly & durable, cannot be used without base board

**Acrylic:**
- "Acrylic Sheets" full section
- "Acrylic vs Laminate Finish" full comparison section
- Definition: high-gloss plastic sheets, glass-like finish
- Properties: mirror-like shine, moisture resistant, easy to clean, many colours
- Shows fingerprints less than gloss-laminate
- More scratch-resistant than laminate
- Seamless finish with minimal joints
- Types: High-Gloss, Matte Acrylic, Solid Acrylic, Acrylic Laminated Sheet, Metallic Acrylic
- Limitations: shows fingerprints and scratches, needs careful handling
- Recommended for kitchens (easy clean, better scratch resistance)

#### 2.3.2 Paint Finishes (`/finishes/paint-finishes`)

**DUCO:**
- "DUCO Paint" full section
- "DUCO vs PU Finish" comparison section
- Definition: spray-paint finish, smooth even surface like car paint
- Available in: matte, satin, gloss
- Properties: custom colours, seamless, modern/minimal look
- Less durable than PU in high-usage areas
- More budget-friendly
- Limitations: costs more than laminates, can scratch, multiple coats needed
- Choose for: clean look on controlled budget

**PU (Polyurethane):**
- "PU Paint" full section
- "DUCO vs PU Finish" comparison section
- Definition: premium multi-layer coating, showroom-quality finish
- Properties: highly durable, scratch-resistant, moisture/heat resistant
- Available in: matte, satin, gloss
- Limitations: costlier, needs skilled application, touch-ups difficult
- Choose for: premium durability and long-term performance

#### 2.3.3 Specialty Finishes (`/finishes/specialty-finishes`)

**Lacquered Glass:**
- "Lacquered Glass Finish" full section
- Definition: painted glass sheet with glossy finish, paint sealed at the back
- Properties: luxurious finish, moisture resistant, easy to clean, modern high-end look
- Used in: kitchen shutters, wardrobes, wall panels, TV units, premium interiors
- Limitations: fragile, heavy, more expensive than laminates/acrylic

**Rattan:**
- "Rattan Finish" full section
- Definition: natural woven material from plant fibres
- Properties: warm earthy look, natural breathable, modern-boho/European style
- Used in: wardrobe shutters, crockery units, shoe cabinets, bed backs, accent furniture
- Limitations: not moisture friendly, needs careful maintenance, mostly decorative
- Avoid wet or humid areas

### 2.4 HARDWARE & ACCESSORIES (`/hardware`)

#### 2.4.1 German vs Indian Accessories (`/hardware/german-vs-indian`)
**PDF Sections Mapped:**
- "Accessories" full comparison section
- German: higher durability, precision engineering, advanced load-bearing, smoother soft-close, better longevity, higher cost
- Indian: good functionality for regular use, cost-effective, wide availability, easy servicing, simpler mechanisms, lower load ratings
- Recommendation: German for long-term/premium, Indian for budget-controlled/investment

#### 2.4.2 Sliding vs Swing Wardrobes (`/hardware/wardrobe-systems`)
**PDF Sections Mapped:**
- "Wardrobe: Sliding vs Swing" full comparison section
- Sliding: needs high-quality channels, poor channels cause wobbling/misalignment, limited access (one side at a time), saves space (compact bedrooms), higher maintenance with inferior hardware
- Swing: hinges more durable and easier to replace, needs clearance space, full access at once, better load distribution, lower long-term maintenance
- Recommendation: sliding only with high-quality hardware; swing more reliable and structurally stable

#### 2.4.3 Full-Height Systems (`/hardware/full-height-systems`)
**PDF Sections Mapped:**
- "Bottom-to-Ceiling (Full Height)" full section
- Advanced Full-Height Technology: full-length rail systems (Russian brand), heavy load-bearing, 100% customization, better alignment/stability/seamless finish, fewer visible joints
- Standard Local Systems: simpler connection methods, more visible joints/profiles, limited adjustability
- Cost guidance: costlier than normal wardrobes; mid-budget → master bedroom only; low budget → avoid; high budget → all bedrooms

### 2.6 COUNTERTOPS (`/countertops`)

#### 2.6.1 Granite vs Quartz (`/countertops/granite-vs-quartz`)
**PDF Sections Mapped:**
- "Granite vs Quartz" full FAQ section
- Granite: natural stone, porous (absorbs slowly without sealer), darker colours preferred (black hides stains), common in Indian households (heavy cooking/spice usage), more economical
- Quartz: engineered stone, non-porous (good-quality brands), wide colour/pattern range, ideal for theme-based/modern kitchens, lighter shades need regular cleaning, nearly double the cost of granite
- Recommendation: quartz if budget permits + reputed brands only (low-quality quartz absorbs liquids); granite for investment projects (cost-effective, durable)
